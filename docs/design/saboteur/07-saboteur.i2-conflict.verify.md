## Confirmed problems (14)

### P1 [high] The BLOCKING finding "the detector cannot fire inside a probe window / A.9 DoD item 1 is unachievable at the stated constants" is factually false. It conflates the FAULT window with the OBSERVATION window.

**Why it breaks:** A.3 step 2 says telemetry trajectory is "sampled every 200ms during DRIVE"; during the whole DRIVE phase, not during the fault. Worse, two of A.3's four REINFORCE criteria are explicitly defined AFTER the fault ends: "Queue depth monotonically increased for >=3 consecutive samples after fault withdrawal" and "Error rate increased after fault withdrawal (the system got worse after the fault ended)." The classifier is designed to observe a post-withdrawal tail, not a 500-2000ms window. A.4's ClassifySignal needs 5+3-1 = 7 samples, spanning 6 intervals = 3.0s (not 3.5s as claimed) at 500ms, and 1.2s at 200ms: trivially available inside any DRIVE phase of a 25-60s world. The conclusion "No probe can ever produce REINFORCE_ACCELERATING at the stated constants" does not follow, yet it is the sole basis for inventing two prothesis.yaml keys and extending A.3's normative probe windows to >=3s. Meanwhile it misses the real, already-filed defect: OQ-006.3 correctly notes A.3's classifier is a BOOLEAN DAMPEN/REINFORCE scheme (four event criteria) that structurally cannot emit the three-valued REINFORCE_ACCELERATING label A.9 DoD item 1 demands. That is a scheme mismatch, not a window-length problem, and longer windows do not fix it.

**Fix:** Withdraw the finding as stated. Replace it with the actual conflict: A.3's two-valued classifier and A.4's three-valued ClassifySignal must be unified so a probe can emit REINFORCE_ACCELERATING (already OQ-006.3). Keep A.3's 200ms trajectory and A.4/A.10's 500ms container-metric rate: OQ-006.2 already resolves them as two different sources, not a contradiction. Do not extend probe windows and do not add config keys.

### P2 [high] Axis-A arithmetic: the probe-sweep budget estimate uses the wrong normative constant, and the rollout cost it relies on is contradicted by the spec's own verdict example.

**Why it breaks:** The analysis writes "~21 probe worlds at 25-60 s each = 9-21 minutes". A.2 states the probe cost normatively: "Cost: ~5-15s per probe." The 25-60s figure is A.5's ROLLOUT cost, a different quantity. Recomputed at A.2's constant: 21 x 5s = 1.75 min, 21 x 15s = 5.25 min; the sweep fits a soak probe budget comfortably and is borderline, not impossible, at gate's 2 min, so the load-bearing premise of its gate argument collapses. Separately §4.6's own verdict has budget.used_s 412 with worlds_run 22 = 18.7 s/world, contradicting A.5's "25-60 seconds each" and therefore also invalidating "10-24 rollouts total" in the MCTS finding (at 18.7 s/world it is ~32; and it also forgot to subtract its own 20% probe budget, which would give 8-19). Finally "~21 probe worlds" treats per-kind-per-node as mandated; A.3's literal text is "For each fault kind in prothesis.yaml -> perturber.allow, generate a micro-probe world" (7 probes), with per-node only in the examples.

**Fix:** Recompute every budget claim against A.2's 5-15s probe cost, and raise the A.2 vs A.5 vs §4.6 cost inconsistency as its own conflict; it is the load-bearing unknown behind OQ-007.1. State per-kind vs per-kind-per-node as a chosen interpretation, not a mandate.

### P6 [high] The stated justification for freezing the Phase 4 `when` trigger in Phase 0 is false, which removes the entire rationale for that over-commitment.

**Why it breaks:** "Reserving the variant now costs one enum and one struct; adding it in Phase 4 is a .thesis format break affecting every stored regression." ### P7 [high] Missed Phase 0 format forcer: nothing joins the history log's absolute nanosecond timebase to the millisecond, DRIVE-relative timebase every other schema uses, and the external oracle contract requires that join.

**Why it breaks:** §4.4 history JSONL uses "t_ns":1725300000000000 (absolute epoch ns). §4.3 fault WINDOWs are "start_ms..end_ms relative to DRIVE start". §4.5 oracle_input hands an external process history_path plus phases in ms (0/42000/45000/50000/55000). §4.6 uses t_ms and first_seen_ms. No document supplies the mapping. I5 makes this load-bearing: an external oracle cannot honour valid_phases if it cannot place a history event in a phase. This is a genuine Phase 0 obligation on internal/recorder and the world/artifact format, and the analysis, whose whole thesis is that I2's tuple is under-specified, asserts "all times are int64 milliseconds on the virtual clock, relative to DRIVE start" in its struct comment without ever supplying the epoch anchor that makes the assertion usable.

**Fix:** Add to the Phase 0 realized layer an explicit anchor (DRIVE start as absolute t_ns, plus the virtual-clock origin), and state the ns -> ms-relative conversion in the schema doc comments and the oracle_input documentation. This displaces one of the speculative Phase 4 fields from the Phase 0 budget.

### P8 [medium] The claim that any float64 breaks the Phase 0 byte-identical round-trip DoD is false; a good rule rests on a fabricated justification.

**Why it breaks:** "It DOES endanger it if any float appears; 0.1 round-trips through float64 unpredictably across encoders." Go's encoding/json emits the shortest representation that round-trips exactly (strconv AppendFloat with -1 precision), so Marshal -> Unmarshal -> Marshal is byte-stable: 0.1 -> "0.1" -> 0.1 -> "0.1". The Phase 0 DoD is about the tool's own serializer round-tripping, not cross-encoder interop, so the cited hazard does not apply. The paragraph also cites A.6's 0.1 and A.10's 1.41 as the risk, but those live in prothesis.yaml and the utility engine, never in the world file. Presenting a false mechanism will make an implementer distrust the rule the moment they test it.

**Fix:** Keep integer milliseconds, justified correctly: integer ms makes ddmin window narrowing, replay comparison and lock hashing exact and order-stable, and avoids floats in any value compared for equality. Delete the encoder claim.

### P9 [medium] The "four independent proofs" that Reading 1 is unimplementable rest on a strawman and a miscited invariant, so "This is decisive on its own" is an overclaim.

**Why it breaks:** Proof 1 says the root node "occurs exactly once and is destroyed by the first action taken from it. There is no undo, no snapshot/restore", concluding "visits is permanently 1 for every node". But MCTS over a real system revisits a node by RE-EXECUTING from BOOT to that node; exactly what A.5 prescribes: "Each rollout executes a real world (not simulated)." Under re-execution visits>1 is attainable with no snapshot and no simulation, so the proof fails. The authority is also miscited: I1 forbids "Fakes and mocks ... in the system under test" and says nothing about checkpointing container state, yet it is cited to rule out snapshot/restore. The position that survives re-execution is precisely the analysis's own "Reading 1.5", so a long refutation arrives where it started while leaving the reader believing a stronger result was proved than was.

**Fix:** Replace the four proofs with the one that holds: a rollout is a whole world because A.5's terminal condition is "Oracle violation detected, or HEAL phase reached without violation, or fault budget exhausted", a full-lifecycle terminal. Drop the visits==1 argument and the I1 citation, and present the conclusion as a chosen reading, which is how OQ-003 already frames it.

### P10 [medium] The early-termination fix nullifies the mechanism it is fixing, and double-patches by also modifying A.6: explicitly normative; for a problem the first patch already solved.

**Why it breaks:** Fix one mandates "HEAL, QUIESCE, ASSERT and TEARDOWN always run", which already removes the wall-clock saving that is the entire point of A.5's early termination. Fix two then changes gamma_duration to "the SCHEDULED world duration rather than the realized one", removing any utility advantage too. With both applied, early termination has zero effect on cost and zero effect on utility: a normative feature reduced to a no-op, unnoticed. Fix two also contradicts A.6's own worked example, which computes the penalty from realized seconds ("2 faults in 30 seconds scores 100x1.5 + 0 - 6 - 3 = 141"; 0.1 x 30 = 3), and it flattens gamma x Duration to a near-constant across rollouts, killing the duration half of the parsimony incentive A.1 point 2 claims as a structural advantage.

**Fix:** Apply only fix one: redefine "terminate the rollout" as "stop DRIVE/PERTURB early, then run HEAL/QUIESCE/ASSERT/TEARDOWN as normal", leave gamma on realized duration so the shortened DRIVE still earns its small reward, and record the single change against OQ-004 rather than modifying A.6.

### P11 [medium] Missed config-surface collision: A.10's utility block cannot express A.6's normative utility function, and its existence conflicts with A.6's no-modification clause.

**Why it breaks:** A.10 exposes a single violation_weight: 100, but A.6's normative switch has five distinct per-class weights (consistency 100, crash 80, safety 60, resource 40, liveness/convergence 20). A user editing prothesis.yaml therefore silently changes the search's objective with no DECISIONS.md entry, against A.6's "It is normative and must not be modified by the implementing agent without recording the change in DECISIONS.md". Under CRUCIBLE §E.2's fault-space-floor logic, lowering these weights is a gate-weakening edit. The analysis reached for lock coverage of the search: block but never noticed the knobs cannot represent the function they configure.

**Fix:** Model utility as a per-class weight map in the config schema and record in DECISIONS.md that A.10's scalar violation_weight is read as the consistency weight with the other four scaled proportionally, or that the field is rejected. Choose one, in writing. Include the utility block in the lock manifest input set when internal/lock is built in Phase 3, not Phase 0.

### P12 [medium] Missed conflict: A.5's action space is filtered only by perturber.budget, never by perturber.allow/deny or perturber.constraints: an I6 hole, not just a bug.

**Why it breaks:** A.5 states: "Action space: The full fault grammar filtered to actions compatible with current perturber.budget constraints (max concurrent faults, max total faults)." §4.2 also carries deny: [io.fill] and constraints: ["never partition more than minority of kv", "pg must be reachable during SEED"]. As written the Saboteur may generate denied kinds and constraint-violating faults. CRUCIBLE §E.1 puts "the allowed fault space" inside the lock manifest, so a search engine ignoring allow/deny is operating outside the locked fault space that I6 exists to police, making its findings unattributable to a locked configuration.

**Fix:** File a new OPEN_QUESTIONS entry against A.5 with the resolution stated: the MCTS action space is filtered by allow, deny, constraints AND budget. No Phase 0 impact; Phase 4 work.

## Missed by the analysis

- The absolute-vs-relative timebase gap: §4.4 history is t_ns absolute epoch, §4.3 fault windows and §4.5 oracle_input phases are ms relative to DRIVE start, §4.6 uses t_ms/first_seen_ms. No document supplies the anchor, yet I5 requires an external oracle to place history events in phases. A genuine Phase 0 obligation on internal/recorder and the world format that the analysis asserts a convention about without supplying.
- topology_variant, one of I2's five tuple elements, is undefined in all three documents (§4.2 has only a fixed harness.nodes list). The analysis freezes it into the Phase 0 struct without noticing a fifth of the tuple it is auditing has no definition, enumeration mechanism, or lock treatment.
- A.5's action space is filtered only by perturber.budget (never allow, deny, or constraints) so the Saboteur may emit denied kinds (io.fill) and constraint-violating faults. Since CRUCIBLE §E.1 puts the allowed fault space in the lock manifest, this is an I6 hole, not merely a bug.
- A.10's single utility.violation_weight cannot express A.6's five per-class weights (100/80/60/40/20), so the config surface cannot represent the normative utility function; and making the constants configurable at all bypasses A.6's "must not be modified ... without recording the change in DECISIONS.md", and is a gate-weakening vector under §E.2.
- A.2 states probe cost as "~5-15s per probe" while A.5 states a real world costs 25-60s and A.3 says probes ARE worlds ("generate a micro-probe world", "Execute each probe world"). A probe cannot be 5-15s if a world is 25-60s. §4.6's own verdict implies a third figure, 18.7 s/world (412s / 22 worlds). Every budget argument in the addendum rests on constants that contradict each other and the base spec.
- The novelty term can dominate the violation term and is non-stationary. beta = 10 per novel template, and §4.6 shows new_templates: 12 = 120 utility, exceeding a high-severity crash (120) and approaching a high-severity consistency violation (150); early rollouts against a fresh system mark nearly all templates novel, so U_avg mixes novelty-inflated early rollouts with saturated later ones and UCT value estimates go stale. The analysis dissects the gamma term in detail and never examines beta.
- OQ-006.4, already on disk and sharper than its own probe finding: A.3 counts "a role change (leader election, failover) occurred within 1 second of fault injection" as REINFORCE evidence, but pausing a Raft leader always causes an election in a correct implementation as much as a buggy one; the criterion carries zero discriminating information for the mandatory fixture. That matters more than the missing no-fault control worlds it did find.
- §4.5 oracle_input passes world_path to every external oracle, and CRUCIBLE §L requires "oracle and world formats must be portable across repos". That makes .thesis a de facto external contract parsed by third-party oracle processes: strengthening the case for getting the Plan/Realized split right in Phase 0, but arguing strongly AGAINST loading it with speculative Phase 4 fields (MCTSPath, TriggerPredicate) that external oracles must parse and ignore.
- Base spec Phase 5 DoD says "Shrunk repro replays with 3/3 certainty" while CRUCIBLE PART 5 says replay "reproduces with high probability, NOT certainty" and must "never claim determinism it does not have". The analysis quotes the second to justify its Replay block but never confronts the first: a direct normative conflict between the two documents over a Phase 5 definition of done.
